Three ways to
colour a graveyard

All three carry the same idea — one colour for the quick, one for the still, and a validated six-hue categorical set that never doubles as anything else. They disagree about what kind of document this is. Switch any screen between them from the palette control in its header.

B · new

Survey

A field survey office. Cool paper, bottle green for the living, pencil grey for the dead. Reads as a document you file, not a console you watch.

The unknown becomes unprinted paper — which is exactly what an incomplete real map looks like, and arguably the truer metaphor. Best for reading prompts and life stories. Green against pencil grey reads as living versus withered — a more literal claim than amber-versus-bone, and a faster one.

Strata

An excavation section. Warm umber ground, ember for the living, chalk for the dead. Older and slower — generations stacking like sediment.

Warmest and most particular. Umber ground suits a register of the dead better than cold teal does. Riskiest: water has to stay blue to read as water, and blue on umber is the one combination here that fights itself.

My preference is still Unlit, on one argument: the knowledge overlay is the most distinctive thing this product does, and it needs a genuinely dark unknown to have any range. Survey is the better document and the worse instrument. Strata is the most characterful and the least legible on the one screen you will look at most.
