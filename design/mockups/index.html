Design mockups · draft 1 · 23 Aug 2026

An instrument for
watching things die well

Life Zone is not a god game and not a colony sim. Nobody gives orders. You build a world, set the rules, and read the record — so the interface is built for reading a record, not commanding an army.

The direction

Two colours carry the whole product: lamplight amber for the quick and cold bone for the still. Every creature is one or the other, and a database of 1,161 lives is mostly the second. That opposition replaces the usual brand accent.

Labels are set in monospace throughout, the way lab equipment is labelled — everything here is a tick, an ID, a coordinate, or a confidence. Prose is reserved for the two places the simulation actually speaks: a creature's stated reasoning, and the story of a life.

The signature

A 672-tick lifespan track appears wherever a creature is named. Zones mark infant, adult and elder; the bar is how much life has been spent; the mark is now, or — in bone — where a life actually ended against the baseline it was owed.

Screens
01 · the live view

World

The map, the speed modes, the deliberation budget, and the inspector. Shows a creature's committed plan with the reasoning it gave, and the beliefs it is acting on.

 02 · the argument

Knowledge

The map as known rather than as it is. Toggle between one creature's beliefs and the community's. Brightness is confidence; the dark is everything nobody has been to.

 03 · the record

A life

Ansa, dead at 658 ticks. Her timeline, her needs, every LLM call she made with the full prompt, and what became of each plan. This is what makes a death explainable.

 04 · the evidence

Reports

Population, lineage, culture, economy, and whether the model is earning its keep. Built to answer the success criteria directly rather than to look busy.
Palette

The six categorical hues are validated against the chart surface — lightness band, chroma floor, colourblind separation, and contrast all pass. They are assigned in fixed order and never cycled.

There are three palettes to choose between — Unlit, Survey and Strata. Every screen carries a switcher in its header, so you can walk the whole app in one and judge it in place rather than from swatches. The tokens below follow whichever is active.

Open for discussion
  1. Three panels may be one too many. The world view runs rail / map / inspector. On a 13" screen the map gets squeezed. The inspector could slide over the map instead of sitting beside it.
  2. Where does the knowledge overlay live? It is mocked as its own screen, but it may work better as an overlay mode on the world view — one toggle rather than a separate destination.
  3. How much prompt to show inline. The record screen shows full prompts. That is right for debugging and probably too much for reading a life. It might want to collapse by default.
  4. The map is a placeholder. Creatures are dots sized by life stage and coloured by household. At 500 creatures on a 512×512 map that may read as noise — worth testing before committing to it.
